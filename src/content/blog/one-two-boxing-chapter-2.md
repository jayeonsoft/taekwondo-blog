The Gangnam district was always meant to be the hardest fight in the game — a champion boss with roughly a million HP who one-shots careless players. Losing to him used to just mean "try again." Now it means something else: a cutscene, a memory-loss beat, and a hard cut into a second chapter, *One-Two Boxing*, that didn't exist a few builds ago.

The pitch was simple — what if losing the big fight wasn't a dead end but a different game? Coach Oh's gloomy basement gym in a Gangnam side street (raised ring, heavy bags, a long mirror, rain on the windows, a half-dead neon sign) became the new hub. The taekwondo belt ladder got a twin: Novice → Amateur → Golden Gloves → Contender → Pro → Champion, earned through gym drills and promotion bouts against named rivals instead of board breaks and forms.

The harder design problem wasn't the gym, it was the lock. Once you're in chapter 2, Seoul Nights' own Start button has to stay locked until you finish it — no deep link, old save, or menu should be able to sneak back into the dojang mid-chapter. Every route that used to lead to the dojang, the district or dojang training now checks the lock first and redirects to the gym instead. "Return to Taekwondo" exists as an escape hatch from the title screen or the gym's own menu, but it says exactly what it will erase before it lets you confirm — resetting all boxing progress while leaving taekwondo progress untouched.

The moveset is a parallel system on purpose, not a reskin: STRAIGHTS, FOOTWORK, HOOKS and COUNTERS replace Punch/Kick/Jump Kick/Spin Kick, with their own mastery gates, their own skill links, and two hidden-route combos (the Dempsey Roll and the Gazelle Punch) for players who go looking. FLOW got swapped out too — boxing runs on a flame meter that burns down on its own and faster if you stand still, so you're incentivized to keep moving and landing clean shots rather than just mashing a multiplier.

Beating Baek "The Wall" in the chapter 2 finale closes the loop: a finale cutscene, the memories come back, and free travel opens up both ways between the dojang and the gym. What started as a joke mockup is now a real second game bolted onto the first one, with its own save file so the two progressions never collide.
